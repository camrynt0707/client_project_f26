During Your Experience!

Navigate Your Active Project

Put your plans into practice through adaptive project management, human-centered fieldwork, and constructive feedback. During this phase, your team will conduct research, coordinate project work, communicate with your sponsor, and respond to new information in real time.

Why Navigating Challenges Matters

The middle of an engaged-learning project is often where the most meaningful growth happens—and where the most difficult challenges emerge.

Real-world projects rarely follow a straight line. Requirements may shift, team dynamics may evolve, partners may face unexpected constraints, and research may challenge your original assumptions.

The goal is not to avoid every obstacle. It is to respond to change thoughtfully, ethically, and collaboratively. By learning to manage expanding scope, navigate mid-project feedback, and realign technical decisions with human needs, you will develop professional skills that extend far beyond a single project.

When you view friction as a natural part of applied learning rather than as a failure, you strengthen your ability to:

  • Adapt plans without losing sight of the project goals
  • Identify and manage risks early
  • Make decisions with incomplete information
  • Communicate challenges early and professionally
  • Balance technical possibilities with human needs
  • Maintain trust during periods of uncertainty
  • Learn from feedback and changing circumstances

Phase 3: Managing & Executing in the Field

Conduct research, monitor progress, and maintain clear communication with your project sponsor or community partner.

What You'll Do

1. Conduct Human-Centered Fieldwork

Use human-centered design methods to observe contexts, learn from stakeholders, and identify patterns that can guide the project.

Your team may:

  • Observe people, activities, environments, and interactions
  • Conduct interviews or other research activities
  • Record findings consistently
  • Separate direct observations from interpretations
  • Identify recurring needs, behaviors, and constraints
  • Translate evidence into actionable insights
  • Revisit assumptions as new information emerges

Approach fieldwork with curiosity and humility. The people closest to the context bring knowledge that your team cannot gain from technical expertise alone.

Important: Follow all applicable course, program, research, privacy, and consent requirements when collecting information from or about people.

2. Coordinate & Track Project Work

Break project goals into manageable milestones and assign clear responsibilities. Use a shared project-management system so everyone can see what is happening and what comes next.

For each task or milestone, identify:

  • The intended outcome
  • The person responsible
  • Supporting team members
  • Dependencies and required inputs
  • The target completion date
  • The current status
  • Risks or barriers
  • Required reviews or approvals

Review the project plan regularly rather than treating it as a one-time document.

3. Keep Sponsors and Partners Informed

Consistent progress updates help maintain trust and give partners meaningful opportunities to shape the work.

A useful project update should include:

  • Work completed since the previous update
  • Current work and upcoming milestones
  • Preliminary findings or decisions
  • Questions requiring partner input
  • Risks, delays, or changing assumptions
  • Proposed adjustments
  • Action items, owners, and deadlines

Be transparent about challenges. Communicating a concern early gives your team and partner time to respond together.

4. Build Effective Feedback Loops

Gather feedback throughout the project, not only after the final deliverable is complete.

Effective feedback cycles include:

  1. Prepare: Identify what you want feedback on and what decisions it will inform
  2. Provide context: Explain the goals, constraints, and current stage of the work
  3. Listen: Record feedback without immediately defending the team's choices
  4. Evaluate: Compare the feedback with research evidence, project goals, and stakeholder needs
  5. Decide: Determine what the team will incorporate, investigate, or set aside
  6. Respond: Tell reviewers how their feedback influenced the work
  7. Document: Record decisions and update the project plan

Not every suggestion must be adopted. However, every meaningful piece of feedback should be considered and addressed respectfully.

5. Manage Scope and Change

“Scope creep” occurs when new requests or expectations expand a project beyond its agreed boundaries without corresponding changes to time, staffing, or resources.

When a change is proposed:

  • Clarify the request and why it matters
  • Compare it with the agreed project scope
  • Assess its effect on workload, timeline, quality, and stakeholders
  • Identify realistic options and tradeoffs
  • Discuss the options with the appropriate decision-makers
  • Document the agreed approach
  • Update the project plan and communicate the change

Avoid making immediate commitments during a meeting. Give your team time to understand the impact before agreeing to new work.

Phase 3 Checklist

Throughout the project, confirm that your team is:

  • Applying appropriate human-centered research methods
  • Following applicable consent, privacy, and research requirements
  • Distinguishing observations from assumptions and interpretations
  • Translating findings into actionable insights
  • Maintaining a shared project plan
  • Assigning owners and deadlines to tasks
  • Monitoring milestones, risks, and dependencies
  • Sending regular progress updates
  • Gathering feedback at meaningful points
  • Documenting decisions and action items
  • Assessing new requests before accepting them
  • Updating the scope and timeline when plans change
  • Asking for support before issues become critical

A Recommended Project Rhythm

Before Each Partner Meeting

  • Review progress against the project plan
  • Gather updates from team members
  • Identify risks, decisions, and unanswered questions
  • Prepare and share and agenda
  • Assign a facilitator and note-taker

During Each Partner Meeting

  • Confirm current priorities
  • Share a concise progress update
  • Present relevant findings or work in progress
  • Gather focused feedback
  • Discuss risks and proposed changes
  • Assign action items and due dates

After Each Partner Meeting

  • Share notes and action items
  • Record decisions and feedback
  • Update the project plan and dashboard
  • Confirm owners and deadlines
  • Follow up on unresolved questions
